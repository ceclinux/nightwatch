# Nightwatch v0.3 "Feet of Clay": bright nights and the best spot

Date: 2026-09-24
Status: approved design, pre-implementation
Owner: rsutcliffe
Builds on: `2026-09-23-nightwatch-design.md` (v0.1), `2026-09-23-dark-sky-sites-design.md` (v0.2). Everything not changed here stays as those specs say.

## 1. Purpose

Nightwatch exists to give a hobbyist a heads-up on when to dust off the telescope, and to give a novice enough information to view from the best spot for the best results (owner, 24 September 2026). v0.3 closes the two gaps in that purpose the product-direction discovery found:

1. From early May to early August at British latitudes the app is silent by design, because the go rule needs three hours inside astronomical darkness and there is none. The Moon and the planets are there all summer, and a smart telescope images them happily. v0.3 adds a bright-night mode so the heads-up keeps coming.
2. The best-spot job is half done. The dark-site cards and the popover's "Clearer sky" line exist, but the line drops the user on the wrong Targets group, and the card does not say how much better the site is than home tonight. v0.3 finishes that path.

Success criteria:

1. On a night at Home with no astronomical darkness, bright-night mode on, and an hour of cloud at or under the limit inside nautical darkness with the Moon or a planet at 15 degrees or higher, the popover shows a bright-night verdict with the window and the Moon and any planets up, and the heads-up notification fires at the usual time with the same content.
2. With bright-night mode off, behaviour on such a night is exactly v0.2.2's ("Nothing to see here", reason line).
3. On a normal dark night, nothing changes: dark mode wins whenever the dark rule can be met.
4. Clicking the popover's "Clearer sky" line opens the Targets window on the Dark sites group with the named site's card visible.
5. Every dark-site card with a forecast shows one comparison line against home: score against score, darkness against the home Bortle class.
6. The site forecast cache holds only files for sites in the current list and none older than 24 hours.

## 2. Research basis

The Product Direction Synthesis (24 September 2026) and its assumption challenge, the owner's rulings on purpose, telescope control, mobile, WeatherKit and logging, and GitHub discussion #1 (open). Summer darkness figures: in northern England the Sun never reaches 18 degrees below the horizon from about 15 May to 30 July, and the three-hour dark rule is never met from about 5 May to 9 August (computed, checked by the challenge). Community evidence for the summer gap: DWARF owners turning to the Moon because darkness comes too late (e-group.uk.net, 2024), and "Fed up with the sun" (same thread). Evidence for the stay-or-go verdict: one r/telescopes post (n=1) plus the owner's purpose statement, which is why the work is a completion of what ships rather than a new feature.

## 4. Design

### 4.1 Bright-night mode

**When it applies.** A night is a bright night when bright-night mode is on and the astronomical-darkness window is shorter than the dark rule's minimum hours (this includes nights with no astronomical darkness at all). Dark mode always wins when the dark rule can be met, so the change never touches a normal night.

**Ephemeris.** `Night` gains `nauticalStart` and `nauticalEnd`, the Sun's minus-12-degree crossings, found with the same `Astronomy_SearchAltitude` calls that find the minus-18-degree crossings, with the same polar-day handling (nil when the Sun never reaches minus 12). `Night.hasNauticalDarkness` mirrors `hasDarkness`.

**Rule.** `BrightSettings { enabled: Bool = false, minHours: Double = 1 }` on `Config.brightNights`, decoded leniently like every other setting. The cloud limit is shared with the dark `GoRule`; the altitude is not: bright targets use the fixed `Planner.brightTargetFloorDeg` (15 degrees), and the dark rule's minimum altitude still governs deep-sky targets only.

**Windows.** `Planner.windows` is reused with the nautical bounds and the bright minimum. An hour counts only when it is clear and a bright target stands at 15 degrees or higher at the hour's centre: the Moon with illumination of 10 percent or more, or a naked-eye planet (Mercury, Venus, Mars, Jupiter, Saturn). Amended by owner ruling on 24 September 2026 from the dark rule's 30 degrees at the window midpoint: a probe of summer 2026 at Home found nothing at 30 degrees mid-window on any of 94 bright nights, and a target at 15 degrees for an hour on about 39 of them, before cloud.

**Plan.** `NightPlan` gains `mode: PlanMode` (`.dark` or `.bright`) and, for bright plans, `brightTargets: [RankedTarget]`, the Moon and the planets up during the primary window, each at its highest altitude in the window (sampled every 30 minutes), the Moon first when it is up and the planets by that altitude. `targets` and `best` are empty in bright mode so nothing deep-sky is ever suggested. The score formula is unchanged except that the Moon term contributes its full 15 points in bright mode (the Moon is the target, not the problem). `Planner.plan` computes the dark plan first; if it does not qualify and the night is a bright night, it computes and returns the bright plan instead. The no-window reason (v0.2.2) applies to whichever mode was tried last, with "nautical darkness" named in the bright case.

**Popover.** The verdict title on a bright night with a window is "Bright night: Moon and planets"; the subtitle is the window as today; the third line lists the bright targets, for example "Moon 62% · Saturn · Jupiter". The header's date line gains " · bright night" after the Bortle class so the state is visible at a glance. The tiles are unchanged. The "Best tonight" row shows the bright targets with their existing photographs. On a bright night without a window the verdict is the existing no-window state with the reason line naming nautical darkness and the bright rule.

**Targets window.** In bright mode the planets group is populated as today; the deep-sky groups show the existing empty state with a one-line note "Bright night: no deep-sky targets suggested". Events, constellations and dark sites are unchanged.

**Alerts.** The same stages and timings. Titles in both wording modes: heads-up "Bright night tonight from HH:MM · Moon 62%, Saturn"; go "Bright night. Clear from HH:MM"; tomorrow preview "Tomorrow looks bright and clear · 2.5 h"; stand-down as today. The notification body lists the bright targets instead of deep-sky names. Quiet hours and the six-hour staleness rule are unchanged.

**Settings.** A "Bright nights" section: a toggle "Moon and planets when there is no proper darkness" (default off) and a stepper "Minimum clear run" (1 to 6 hours, step 0.5, default 1), with a caption explaining that the mode applies only when the dark rule cannot be met.

**Dark sites on a bright night.** Site plans use the same mode selection as home, so the "Clearer sky" line and the cards compare like with like.

### 4.2 Best-spot completion

**Landing on the card.** `Store` gains `@Published var targetsRequest: TargetsRequest?` where `TargetsRequest { section: BrowserSection, siteID: String? }`. The popover's "Clearer sky" line sets it to the Dark sites section with the site's id, then opens the window. `TargetsView` consumes the request on appear and on change: it sets the section, scrolls the grid to the card (a `ScrollViewReader` keyed by site id), and clears the request. The "All targets →" button and the gear are unchanged.

**The comparison line.** `DarkSiteCard` shows one line under the distance line: "Score 61 vs 37 at home · Dark, home Bortle 5" when the card has a forecast and home has a plan, or "Dark, home Bortle 5" when it does not. The band comes from the card's `band` or, for certified places with a stated Bortle class, "Bortle 2"; the home class is the active site's Bortle setting. Colour: the score pair uses the accent when the site beats home by the existing 20-point margin, dim otherwise.

**The popover line.** Gains the band in brackets after the site name: "Clearer sky 41 km E: Yorkshire Dales (Dark), clear 00:00–04:57 →". No other change.

### 4.3 Housekeeping

**Site cache pruning.** After each `recomputeDarkSites`, delete every file in `Caches/Nightwatch/sites/` whose name is not `<id>.json` for an id in the current `darkSites`, and every file older than 24 hours. Pruning never blocks the main actor for longer than a directory listing; on any error it does nothing.

**Away-line landing.** Covered by 4.2.

### 4.6 Aurora alerts (added 24 September 2026)

**Purpose.** A heads-up when the aurora may be visible from the active site right now and the local sky is clear enough to see it, because a UK aurora alert means nothing under cloud.

**Source.** AuroraWatch UK (Lancaster University), current status, API version 0.2: `https://aurorawatch-api.lancs.ac.uk/0.2/status/current-status.xml`, an XML document whose `site_status` element carries `status_id` = `green`, `yellow`, `amber` or `red` and whose `updated/datetime` gives the time. Free, no key or registration; terms: non-commercial use only, attribution to AuroraWatch UK, the status shown with its correct colour and name, and each client polls no more often than every 3 minutes (AuroraWatch UK API page, read 24 September 2026). These terms match Nightwatch's existing non-commercial footing with 7Timer. The owner's constraint is free or open data only; AuroraWatch UK is free. NOAA SWPC's Kp forecast and OVATION grid are also free JSON, but their licence for these feeds is not stated on the pages read, so they are not used in v0.3.

**Levels (AuroraWatch UK's own descriptions).** Green: unlikely to be visible anywhere in the UK. Yellow: may be visible by eye from Scotland and by camera from Scotland, northern England and Northern Ireland. Amber: likely visible by eye from Scotland, northern England and Northern Ireland, possibly elsewhere. Red: likely visible by eye and camera anywhere in the UK.

**Rule.** An aurora notification fires when all of these hold: aurora alerts are enabled; the status is at or above the chosen threshold (default amber, choice yellow, amber or red); the Sun is at least 12 degrees below the horizon at the active site (between nautical dusk and dawn, or all night where there is no nautical twilight but the Sun is still down: the rule uses the Sun's altitude now, not the night's bounds); the forecast hour containing now has cloud at or under the go rule's cloud limit; and no aurora notification has fired this night at this level or higher. A rise to a higher level fires again. Quiet hours apply as for every other alert.

**Polling.** Only while aurora alerts are enabled and the Sun is at least 12 degrees down: every 5 minutes, one request, cached last status on failure. No polling in daylight.

**Presentation.** Notification title "Aurora alert: amber · clear at Home now"; body "AuroraWatch UK reports amber. Cloud 15% this hour." The popover shows one line under the verdict while the status is at or above the threshold: "Aurora: amber (AuroraWatch UK)" in the level's colour, whatever the sky. Settings gains an "Aurora" section: a toggle "Alert me to aurora when the sky is clear" (default off), a threshold picker (Yellow, Amber, Red; default Amber), and a caption naming AuroraWatch UK and the rule. About and NOTICE gain the AuroraWatch UK acknowledgement.

**Tests.** Parsing the live XML shape for each level and the time; the rule's truth table (disabled, below threshold, Sun too high, cloudy, already alerted at this level, higher level refires); the alert state resets on a new night; settings decode leniently.

### 4.4 Failure handling

Bright-night mode off: every code path is v0.2.2's. No nautical twilight (polar summer): no bright window, the existing no-darkness verdict. Ephemeris failure on a bright night: the plan falls back to the dark computation as today. A `targetsRequest` for a site no longer in the list: the section still changes, no scroll, the request is cleared. Cache pruning errors are ignored.

### 4.5 Tests

Ephemeris: nautical crossings for Home on 20 June (present) and 24 September (present, earlier than the astronomical ones), and nil for Tromsø on 20 June. Planner: a July night at Home with clear hours and the Moon and Saturn up yields a bright plan with a window and the Moon first in `brightTargets`; a June night with nothing at 15 degrees yields no window; a September night with a qualifying dark window yields a dark plan even with bright mode on; bright mode off yields v0.2.2 behaviour on the June night; the score's Moon term is 15 in bright mode; the no-window reason names nautical darkness on a bright night. Config: `brightNights` decodes from a file without the key to the defaults. Alerts: the bright heads-up title and body. Store logic that is pure enough to test (`TargetsRequest` equality, prune selection given a file list and ids) is tested in SkyCore; the pruning file operation is exercised by a temp-directory test. Views: build only.
